Art direction:
A lovingly handcrafted, stylized miniature world at blue hour. Sculpted diorama architecture, tactile painted wood, softly bevelled stone and tiles, simple elegant shapes, beautifully controlled physically plausible lighting with a subtle painted finish. High-end whimsical realism with mature taste. Not photorealism, not pixel art, not ink-wash, not sepia parchment, not anime character illustration. Deep indigo and petrol blue shadows, muted jade vegetation, weathered cream walls, restrained terracotta roofs and warm amber lantern light. Saturated accents are carefully controlled. A sophisticated boutique strategy-adventure game, peaceful but full of possibility.

Main visual:
A small ancient East-Asian-inspired hillside river city viewed from an elevated three-quarter perspective at dusk. Architecture climbs a rocky headland from a quiet river harbour to a walled upper district and a graceful watchtower. An arched bridge leads toward the city gate, with a clear readable path winding through a modest market square and up the hill. Small lanterns and shop windows light up in irregular human patterns. A few tiny travellers cross the bridge, a porter carries goods, two guard figures walk near the gate, and one small boat with a warm cabin light drifts under the bridge. The inhabitants are small and never featured as portraits. Dark wooded hills and a misty river bend frame the scene. One or two warm cloud bands retain the last light after sunset. Trees have appealing grouped canopies, roofs have sculpted grouped tiles rather than overwhelming tiny detail, water has calm graphic reflections and ripples.
The entire composition tells a story of a world that goes on living after dark. A welcoming place with layers to explore. No battle, no hero character in front, no throne, no weapon display, no armies, no dramatic apocalypse, no floating island cutout, no toy-like plastic gloss.
